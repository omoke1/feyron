( The problem )

The gap always lands
at 7:43.

A monitor calls in sick, a new service opens, a shift is half-covered — and a coordinator starts scrolling group chats, guessing who's free, who has the certificate, who can physically get there by four. Coverage runs on memory and luck.

Workee replaces the scramble with a system. It reads the pending need, cross-checks requirements, availability, distance and history against the company's own pool of temporary and part-time workers, and recommends exactly who to invite. No new marketplace, no strangers: your bolsa, ordered.

It sees everything a group chat can't sort.

Not who's free — who fits.

Four signal cards show what the model actually weighs: who can truly work that slot, who holds the language or certification, who can reach the centre without improvising, who has accepted — and shown up — before. It all lands in one plain-Spanish recommendation card: invite these people first, and here's why.

Invitación enviada16:01 · cohorte 1 · 6 personas

Four medallions, one pipeline.

The flow section compresses the whole machine into four watercolor medallions: the AI understands the pending coverage, ranks the workers, sends invitations without noise — first to the cohort that fits best, escalating only if nobody accepts — and leaves evidence behind. Numbered chips in the product's four accent colors carry the sequence.

The worker's side is a feed, not a form.

On the phone, opportunities read like cards in a feed — each with its state chip, centre, schedule and distance: invitación, nuevo hueco, refuerzo, cerca de ti. The worker understands the context and signs up with one tap; coordination watches the coverage advance in real time instead of reconstructing WhatsApp threads.

Cobertura cerrada18:04 · fichaje GPS · horas · valoración

Closed means proven.

Workee deliberately isn't an HR suite — no payroll, no holidays. The product grid states its narrow obsession: automatic eligibility, a ranking that arrives with reasons, cohort invitations, a coverage map, and GPS-stamped evidence binding acceptance, hours and rating to the shift. Then it learns which centres are hard and who actually says yes.

( Under the hood )

Operational signals,
not org charts.

01 · Matching

Five signals per match

Availability, requirements, distance, history and conflicts feed every ranking — and each recommendation ships with its reasons: why this person, what's the risk, what's left to confirm.

02 · Two sides

One state, everywhere

The coordinator's web panel and the worker's mobile app read from the same live coverage state — invitations, acceptances and open holes update in real time, so nobody reconstructs a chat thread.

03 · Evidence

Proof, not promises

GPS clock-in, incidents, hours and ratings stay bound to each coverage as a queryable trail — and an MCP endpoint exposes it all to AI agents, so the ops data works as hard as the ops team.
